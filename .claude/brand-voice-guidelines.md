## Executive Summary

The Gentry Lab is Cambodia's industrial development advisory platform, and its voice is built entirely around one credibility claim: **we've done the work, not just studied it**. Since 2014, GentryLab has advised, designed, permitted, and delivered over $500M in industrial projects across Cambodia — and every piece of copy on the site leans on that lived, field-level experience rather than market-report abstraction.

The core personality is the **practitioner, not the consultant**. Where competitors and generic market reports tell an investor *where* to build, GentryLab shows *how* — down to which CDC officer to meet first, which provincial EDC office actually has spare grid capacity, and which permit filed in the wrong order restarts the whole sequence. The tone is direct, numbers-first, and unafraid of specificity: dollar figures, week counts, ministry names, and permit codes appear constantly instead of vague reassurance.

Content should feel like it was written by someone who has personally sat across the table from a CDC officer, not by a marketing team paraphrasing a PDF. Confidence is earned through precision, not adjectives.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|--------|------------|
| **Field-tested** — every claim traces back to a real project, permit, or negotiation | **Theoretical** — we don't cite reports we haven't lived |
| **Specific** — dollar figures, week counts, ministry names, permit codes | **Vague** — no "significant savings" or "streamlined process" without a number attached |
| **Direct** — states the risk plainly, even when it's uncomfortable | **Reassuring for its own sake** — we don't soften a real risk to close a sale |
| **Execution-focused** — "how," not just "where" | **Market-report generic** — we don't lead with macro trends divorced from action |
| **Transparent about certainty** — estimates are labeled as estimates | **Overconfident** — we don't present an estimate as a fact |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Field-tested
- **What it means**: Every claim on the platform is backed by a specific, named precedent — a permit sequence GentryLab has actually navigated, a substation GentryLab has actually negotiated, a factory GentryLab has actually delivered.
- **How it shows up**: Named case studies with real dollar figures (Techo Airport Industrial Zone — $16M+ roof scope; Zinus Cambodia Factory — 65,000 m², 3MW EDC power secured); a methodology built from "over a decade inside the sector, not from a desk."
- **What to avoid**: Never present a general industry claim as GentryLab's own experience. If it's from secondary research, cite the source explicitly (CBRE, JETRO, World Bank) rather than blending it into first-person claims.
- **Evidence**: "We don't tell you where. We show you how." (About page); "Built from lived execution, not secondary research." (About page)
- **Confidence**: High

#### Specific
- **What it means**: Numbers do the persuading — not adjectives. Every section prefers a real range or figure over a qualitative claim.
- **How it shows up**: "EDC industrial tariff: $0.12–$0.18/kWh — new substation: 8–24 months"; "Only 30% of Cambodian industrial land has hard LMAP title"; "9 separate ministry approvals — order matters, most investors get it wrong."
- **What to avoid**: Don't say "significant cost savings" — say "$0.04/kWh differential = $840K over 20 years on 3MW." Don't say "fast permitting" — say "8–11 months done correctly, 18–30 months done wrong."
- **Evidence**: Hero stat copy across About and Home pages; AI Advisor system prompt ("Be direct... use specific numbers throughout").
- **Confidence**: High

#### Direct
- **What it means**: Risk is named plainly, including risks that would be more comfortable to omit (land title fraud, permit sequencing traps, EBA withdrawal risk).
- **How it shows up**: "⚠️ Key Risk" is a mandatory section in every AI-generated brief; About page names "The Gap" directly: reports "produced by people who have never permitted a factory, negotiated an EDC connection."
- **What to avoid**: Don't bury a real risk in hedged language. State it, then explain how to mitigate it — don't just reassure.
- **Evidence**: "Never purchase land before a CDC pre-consultation. Some parcels adjacent to protected areas or waterways have zero pathway to a construction permit regardless of their title type." (Field note, Stage 01)
- **Confidence**: High

#### Execution-focused
- **What it means**: The platform's differentiator is the *how* — the specific sequence, the specific office, the specific document — not general market commentary.
- **How it shows up**: The GIDF 9-Stage Framework itself; permit sequencing described as "GentryLab's highest-value advisory service"; "we show you how" is the literal tagline opposite "we don't tell you where."
- **What to avoid**: Don't lead with macro-economic scene-setting (labor costs vs. Vietnam, EBA trade access) without connecting it to a concrete next action.
- **Evidence**: "9-stage GIDF methodology covering every step" (About); Home page hero: "What does it actually take to develop industrial land in Cambodia?"
- **Confidence**: High

#### Transparent about certainty
- **What it means**: The platform explicitly distinguishes verified data from estimates, and says so out loud rather than presenting everything with uniform confidence.
- **How it shows up**: "Data illustrative · Verify before investment decision" (map disclaimer); "Each field is labeled by method... estimated (straight-line) is not the same as measured (road distance)" (Methodology page); source citations added to chart data with explicit "TGL Research estimate, benchmarked to..." framing when hard data isn't available.
- **What to avoid**: Don't present a directional estimate as if it were a verified figure. When uncertain, say so and give a defensible range instead of a fabricated precise number.
- **Evidence**: "Industrial decisions need data you can defend. Every figure on the platform carries its source, the date it was checked, and how certain it is — and we label estimates as estimates." (Methodology page)
- **Confidence**: High

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
"We don't tell you where to build in Cambodia. We show you how — because we've built $500M+ worth of it ourselves."

Variations observed:
- "Built from $500M+ of delivered industrial projects in Cambodia." (Home hero)
- "GentryLab is Cambodia's Industrial Development Advisory Platform — built from over a decade inside the sector, not from a desk." (About page)
- "The Gentry Lab Advisor... generates structured, decision-ready investment briefs" (AI Advisor system prompt)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Execution over research**
   - Core idea: Market reports tell you where; GentryLab shows you how, because it has actually done the work.
   - When to use: Hero sections, About page, any comparison against competitors or generic market intelligence.
   - Example phrasing: "Most industrial market intelligence is produced by people who have never permitted a factory, negotiated an EDC connection, or sat across a table with a CDC officer."

2. **Sequencing is the hidden risk**
   - Core idea: The biggest cost in Cambodia industrial development isn't the individual step — it's doing the steps in the wrong order.
   - When to use: Permit Navigation content, framework stage pages, AI Advisor briefs.
   - Example phrasing: "Done correctly: 8–11 months. Done incorrectly: 18–30 months. Permit sequencing is GentryLab's highest-value advisory service."

3. **Data with a provenance trail**
   - Core idea: Every number on the platform is traceable — sourced, dated, and labeled by confidence — so investors can defend the figures to their own board.
   - When to use: Methodology page, chart captions, AI Advisor outputs.
   - Example phrasing: "Every figure on the platform carries its source, the date it was checked, and how certain it is — and we label estimates as estimates."

### Competitive Positioning
- vs. generic market reports: We've lived the process; they've summarized secondary sources.
- vs. doing it without local advisory: Investors who arrive with a site already chosen "typically spend 14 additional months correcting location decisions made without systematic data."
- vs. status quo (going it alone): 9 separate ministry approvals, done in the wrong order, can turn an 8-month permit sequence into a 30-month one.

| AI Advisor briefs | Medium-High | Medium | High | Structured, numbers-dense, tables over prose |
| Methodology / data trust pages | High | Low-Medium | Medium | Precision about certainty, no oversell |
| News / research summaries | Medium | Medium | Medium | Factual, source-attributed, no editorializing |
| Contact / advisory CTA | Medium | Medium | Low | Direct ask, no filler |
| Field notes / risk warnings | Medium | Medium-High | Medium-High | Name the risk plainly, then the mitigation |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Hero / Landing Copy
- **Overall tone**: Confident, provocative question or claim, immediately grounded in a real number.
- **Opening approach**: Ask what it *actually* takes, or state a track-record figure ($500M+, 9 stages, 260+ sites), before elaborating.
- **Do's**: Lead with the platform's lived-experience proof point; keep sentences short.
- **Don'ts**: Don't open with generic Cambodia market commentary that any research firm could write.
- **Example**: "What does it actually take to develop Industrial land in Cambodia?"

#### AI Advisor Briefs
- **Overall tone**: Analyst-grade, structured, no fluff — reads like an internal memo from someone who's run this exact process before.
- **Do's**: Use tables for comparisons and cost breakdowns; cite specific ministries, provinces, and dollar ranges; always include a named "Key Risk" and a specific "GentryLab Assessment" recommendation.
- **Don'ts**: Never fabricate a specific project, investor name, or dollar figure that isn't sourced or clearly framed as an estimate.
- **Example**: "Based on your inputs, Kampong Speu SEZ is the strongest match. Start with a CDC pre-consultation before committing to land."

#### Methodology / Data Trust
- **Overall tone**: Almost clinical in its honesty about what is and isn't verified.
- **Do's**: Label every figure's source and confidence; distinguish "estimated" from "measured" explicitly.
- **Don'ts**: Don't smooth over data gaps with confident-sounding language.
- **Example**: "Data illustrative · Verify before investment decision."

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| GIDF / GentryLab Industrial Development Framework | The proprietary 9-stage methodology | "our process" | "9-stage GIDF methodology covering every step" |
| QIP (Qualified Investment Project) | CDC's investment incentive status | "tax break status" | "QIP status unlocks up to 9 years corporate tax exemption" |
| MISTI | Current name for the industrial/operating licence ministry | "MIH" (outdated) | "MoE ECC → MISTI operating licence → CDC QIP" |

### Preferred Terms
| Term | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| "Decision-grade" | Describing research/data quality | "Decision-grade research for foreign investors." |
| "Field-verified" | Describing GIDF content, as opposed to desk research | "All data is Cambodia-specific, field-verified." |
| "SEZ" (not "special zone") | Referring to Special Economic Zones | "9 corridors · 260+ sites" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| "MIH" | Outdated ministry name (superseded by MISTI) | "MISTI" |
| "significant" / "substantial" (unquantified) | Vague — violates the Specific voice attribute | Replace with the actual number or range |
| "seamless" / "streamlined" | Generic SaaS-marketing language, doesn't fit the practitioner voice | Describe the actual mechanism (e.g., "in-zone customs clearance same day vs. 3–5 days outside") |
